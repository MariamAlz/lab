How far was that lightning?

Light reaches you almost instantly, but thunder travels at the speed of sound, about a kilometre every three seconds. Tap when you see the flash and again when you hear the thunder. Log a few strikes and the page tells you whether the storm is coming closer or moving away.

    The 30/30 rule

    30 seconds: if the thunder comes less than 30 seconds after the flash, the storm is within about 10 km. That's close enough to be struck. Go indoors now. 30 minutes: wait 30 minutes after the last thunder you hear before going back outside. Lightning often strikes behind the rain, after the sky looks like it's clearing.

    Storm safety in the UAE
    • Stay out of wadis and low roads. Rain on the Hajar mountains can send a flash flood down a dry wadi in minutes, even where it isn't raining.
    • Get off the beach, out of the water and off open desert, golf courses and rooftops. You don't want to be the tallest thing around.
    • A building or a hard-topped car is safe. Close the windows and don't touch metal parts. A tent, majlis shade or bus shelter is not safe.
    • Don't shelter under a lone tree or palm, or next to light poles and fences.
    • Don't drive through water on the road. 30 cm of moving water can float a car. Turn round if you can't see the road markings.
    • Follow the National Center of Meteorology (NCM) for yellow, orange and red alerts, and police alerts on your phone. In an emergency call 999.
    • Indoors, unplug sensitive electronics and avoid corded phones and showers while the storm is overhead.

    Sound is faster in warm air because the air molecules are already moving faster. At 0 °C it travels at 331 m/s, at 40 °C about 355 m/s. On a summer night in the UAE that's a 7% difference from the textbook value, which this page adjusts for. Your reaction time adds some error, so treat each distance as give or take a few hundred metres.
