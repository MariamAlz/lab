Space weather now.

The Sun constantly throws out light, particles and magnetic fields. When it gets stormy, the upper atmosphere is disturbed, and that can bend GPS signals, black out long-range radio and drag on satellites. This reads NOAA's live space weather data and explains what today's levels mean.

    Why the UAE gets its own kind of GPS trouble. The UAE sits under the northern edge of a band of extra-dense ionosphere that follows the magnetic equator. In the hours after sunset, especially in spring and autumn and in years near solar maximum, this layer can break into bubbles that make satellite signals twinkle, a bit like stars twinkling through hot air. Phones barely notice, but precise GPS used for surveying, farming, drones and aviation can lose accuracy, even on days when Kp is low.

    Kp is a 0 to 9 score of how disturbed Earth's magnetic field is, updated every three hours from magnetometers around the world. NOAA turns Kp 5 to 9 into storm levels G1 to G5. X-ray flares (classes A, B, C, M, X, each ten times stronger than the last) affect the sunlit side of Earth within minutes and set the radio blackout level R1 to R5.

    This page is for general interest and learning. For aviation, marine or survey work, follow official NOTAMs, notices and your equipment's own integrity warnings. Data from NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center, refreshed every ten minutes.
