Arabic rhythm machine.

Arabic music runs on iqa'at: rhythm cycles made of two main drum sounds. Dum is the deep stroke in the middle of the drum, and tak is the sharp one at the rim. Pick a cycle to hear it on a synthesised darbuka and riq, slow it down to learn it, or tap in your own.

Reading the cycle. Each dot is one step, going clockwise from the top. Big gold dots are dums, teal dots are taks, and the small marks are rests. The longer ticks show the beats you'd count or clap. Drummers fill the gaps with quick light strokes, so what you hear in a song is busier than this skeleton.

Rhythms of the Gulf. Emirati forms such as ayyala and liwa are played by groups on several large drums, frame drums and cymbals, with parts that lock together. They don't reduce well to one darbuka line, and a reliable notation for them is hard to find, so they're not presets here. Khaleeji, the common Gulf pop rhythm, is. Try building your own in the editor with "My rhythm".

Want a melody on top? Open the Maqam explorer in another tab and play a scale while the rhythm runs. All sounds here are synthesised in your browser: the dum is a falling sine wave, the tak is filtered noise, and the riq jingles are short bursts of high, metallic noise.

Patterns follow the skeleton forms in the Iqa'at library, Michael Frishkopf's durub chart (University of Alberta) and MaqamWorld. Players and regions vary them.
