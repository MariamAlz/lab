Did I just feel that?

The UAE itself is quiet, but the mountains of southern Iran across the Gulf are one of the most active earthquake zones on Earth. Big ones there are sometimes felt in Dubai towers. This lists recent earthquakes in the region and estimates how strongly each one might have reached you.

    Earthquakes

      How the estimate works. Shaking at your spot depends mostly on two things: how big the earthquake was, and how far away you are. Each step up in magnitude releases about 32 times more energy. The shaking then fades with distance, quickly at first and then more slowly. We use a published formula (Atkinson, Worden and Wald, 2014) that turns magnitude and distance into a shaking level on the Modified Mercalli scale, from I (not felt) to X (very destructive). It counts the quake's depth as part of the distance.

      Why towers feel more. Distant large earthquakes send out slow, rolling waves. Tall buildings sway in step with them, so people on high floors in Dubai or Abu Dhabi can feel a quake that people on the ground miss. This is an estimate, not a measurement, and local ground and buildings change it a lot. The UAE's National Center of Meteorology runs its own seismic network and reports small local quakes that may not appear here. Data from the USGS, refreshed every five minutes.
