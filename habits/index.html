      How the Ramadan times work. Fajr is when the sun is 18.2° below the horizon before dawn, the angle used for UAE timetables, and that is when suhoor ends. Maghrib, and iftar, is sunset. Both come from the sun's position for your latitude and longitude on that date. The Ramadan dates come from your browser's built-in Umm al-Qura calendar, so the start can differ by a day from the moon-sighting announcement. Check your local mosque or the official UAE timetable, which uses its own settings and can differ by a few minutes.

      Streaks. A daily streak counts days in a row up to today, and today doesn't break it until it's over. A weekly streak counts weeks in a row (Monday to Sunday) where you hit your target.
