Arabic root explorer.

Most Arabic words grow from a root of three letters. Pour the root into a pattern (وزن) and you get a word with a predictable kind of meaning: a doer, a place, a tool, a plural. Pick a root to see the real words made from it, then test yourself by guessing the root of a word.

Strip the extra letters that patterns add: the م of places and participles, the ت and ا of verb forms, the س and ت of اسْتَفْعَلَ, and the و or ي of long vowels. What is left, in order, is usually the root.

How patterns work, with ف ع ل

Grammarians describe every pattern with the dummy root ف ع ل ("to do"). The first root letter goes where ف is, the second where ع is, the third where ل is. Everything else in the pattern stays. So كاتِب is فاعِل with ك ت ب poured in: the one who writes.

Roots with و, ي or a hamza (ء) are called weak or hamzated. Their letters change shape or drop out in many patterns, as in قالَ from ق و ل or بائِع from ب ي ع. A few are in the list, marked, but the pattern rules on this page are written for sound roots.
