Sunscreen

How it's worked out. Burning starts at one "minimal erythema dose": about 200 J/m² of sunburn-weighted UV for skin type I, 250 for II, 300 for III, 450 for IV, 600 for V and 1,000 for VI. Each point of UV index is 0.025 W/m² of that light. So at UV 10, type II skin collects 250 J/m² in 1,000 seconds: about 17 minutes. Sunscreen multiplies the time, but most people put on half the tested amount, which gives roughly the square root of the SPF. SPF 30 behaves like SPF 5 or 6.

Vitamin D. Skin makes vitamin D from the same UV that burns. For lighter skin, a few minutes of midday sun on arms and legs is usually enough; darker skin needs several times longer. When the UV is under 3, very little is made. There's no need to burn to get it.

What's approximate. UV comes from the Open-Meteo forecast, not a meter beside you, and clouds, dust and shade change it minute to minute. Skin types and burn doses vary a lot between people, and darker skin can still burn and still gets skin damage. Sunscreen wears off with sweat, towels and water whatever the label says. This is an estimate, not medical advice. If you're on medicines that make you sun-sensitive, ask your pharmacist.
