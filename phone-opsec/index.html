Quick 10-minute essentials

    Why restarting matters. Right after a restart, before you type your passcode for the first time, your phone is in its strongest state. The keys that decrypt most of your data are not in memory yet, so a thief or a forensic tool has very little to work with. Once you unlock it, those keys stay loaded until the next restart. That is why iPhones (since iOS 18.1) and Android phones (through Google Play services and Advanced Protection) now restart themselves after about three days locked and unused, and why turning your phone fully off before handing it over is a good habit.

    Most phone takeovers are not clever hacks. They are a passcode seen over a shoulder, a code read off the lock screen, a SIM moved to another phone, or a link in a message. The Everyday level closes those gaps. Settings menus move between versions, so if a name differs on your phone, search for it in the Settings search bar. This is general guidance, not a guarantee. If you think you are being targeted, speak to a security professional.
