      Known scam types in the UAE

      Fake traffic fines, Salik and parking

      A text says you have an unpaid fine or toll and must pay within hours to avoid black points. The link goes to a copy of a government payment page. Real fines are checked in the official apps and websites of RTA, the police or your emirate's transport authority. Type the address yourself.

      Parcel held for a small customs fee

      "Your parcel is on hold, pay AED 9.75." The small amount makes you relax, but the page steals your card details and then the OTP. Couriers show real fees in their own app, and you can check the tracking number on their official site.

      Your bank account or card is blocked

      A message or call claims your account is suspended and asks you to "verify" or "update" details through a link, or to read out a code. Banks never ask for your PIN, CVV, password or OTP. Call the number on the back of your card.

      Fake police, prosecution or "money laundering" calls

      Someone in a uniform on a video call says your Emirates ID is linked to a crime and asks you to transfer money to "clear your name", and to tell no one. Police don't handle cases over WhatsApp or ask for money transfers. Hang up and call the police yourself.

      "Hi Mum, this is my new number"

      A message from an unknown number pretends to be your son, daughter or relative with a broken phone, then asks for urgent money. Call the old number or ask something only they would know before sending anything.

      Part-time jobs and "task" scams

      Earn AED 500 a day by liking videos or rating hotels. You get paid small amounts at first, then must "top up" to unlock bigger tasks and lose it all. Real employers never ask you to pay to work.

      Investment and crypto groups

      WhatsApp or Telegram groups show screenshots of huge profits and a "mentor" offers guaranteed returns. Withdrawals then need "taxes" or "fees". Guaranteed high returns don't exist. Check that a firm is licensed by the UAE financial regulators.

      Prizes, raffles and loyalty points

      You won a car, cash or points that "expire today". To claim it you pay a fee or enter card details. If you didn't enter, you didn't win.

      WhatsApp account takeover

      A friend's account (already stolen) asks you to forward a 6-digit code "sent by mistake". That code is your WhatsApp registration code, and it hands them your account. Never share it. Turn on two-step verification in WhatsApp settings.

      Rental and holiday home deposits

      A great flat at a low price, but the "landlord" is abroad and wants a deposit before a viewing. Only pay after viewing, through a registered agent, with a contract registered in your emirate's tenancy system.

      Marketplace "buyer" sends a payment link

      You sell something online and the buyer sends a link "to receive the money". It asks for your card details. You never need to enter card details to receive money.

      Fake visa, Emirates ID and job-permit services

      Sites that look official charge extra or steal data when you renew a visa or ID. Use the official ICP and government apps, or registered typing centres.

      How it works. The page runs about 25 rules on your text, in English and Arabic, looking for the building blocks almost every scam uses: a reason to panic, a deadline, a request for money or codes, and a link or number to act on. Links are taken apart to find the real domain, then compared with genuine UAE domains and common look-alike tricks. A clean result doesn't prove a message is safe. New scams change their wording all the time. If in doubt, contact the company or person through a number or app you already trust.
