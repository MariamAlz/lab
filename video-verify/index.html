Video verification kit.

Most fake videos aren't edited. They're real footage from another year or another country, reposted with a new caption. The fastest way to catch that is to reverse-search still frames. This pulls out the key frames, reads the metadata the file still carries, and walks you through the other checks. The video is never uploaded.

Drop a video file here, orMP4, MOV and WebM work best. Save the video from the app first; screen recordings work too.

Phones write a creation time into MP4 and MOV files (the mvhd box), and often the app or encoder name and sometimes a GPS position. WhatsApp, Instagram, X and TikTok re-encode uploads and wipe most of it, so an empty result is normal. A date that is present can still be wrong: it comes from the device clock, and it is stored in UTC.

Key frames

The search buttons open Google Lens, Bing, Yandex or TinEye in a new tab and copy the frame to your clipboard. These sites need you to upload the picture yourself, so paste it there (Ctrl+V or long-press, Paste) or drag in the PNG. This page uploads nothing. Yandex is often best for buildings and faces of places outside Western Europe; TinEye sorts matches by date, which helps find the first copy.

Verification checklist
    How scene detection works

    The page plays through the video in small steps and shrinks each frame to 48 × 27 pixels. It compares the colour histogram and the pixels with the previous step. A cut to a new shot changes both a lot; a person walking changes them a little. When the change passes the sensitivity level, that frame is kept at full size. Lower sensitivity catches slow pans; higher keeps only hard cuts.

    Reverse search works best on frames with something distinctive: a skyline, a sign, a shopfront, a mountain ridge. Crop out captions and logos added by reposters before searching, since they can make the engine match the repost rather than the original. Your checklist ticks are saved only in this browser.
