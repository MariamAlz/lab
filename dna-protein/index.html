All six reading frames

One letter per amino acid. * is a stop codon. The longest run without a stop is usually the real gene.

The codon wheel

Read from the middle out: first letter, second, third. The current codon lights up as the ribosome reads it.

Why sickle cell happens. In the HBB sample, codon 7 is GAG, which codes for glutamic acid. Change the middle A to T and it reads GTG, valine. One letter out of more than 400 in the gene. Glutamic acid carries a charge and likes water; valine doesn't. The changed haemoglobin sticks together when oxygen is low, and red blood cells bend into a sickle shape. It's often written Glu6Val, because the first methionine is cut off the finished protein.

Four kinds of point change. Silent: the codon changes but the amino acid doesn't, since most amino acids have several codons. Missense: one amino acid swaps for another. Nonsense: the codon becomes a stop, so the protein ends early. Frameshift: adding or removing letters (not in threes) shifts every codon after it, which usually scrambles the rest.

The HBB sample is the start of the coding sequence from the reference record NM_000518, the same in every human reference. Real genes also have introns that are cut out of the mRNA before translation. Here the sample already has them removed. Classifications are a teaching model, not a genetic test.
