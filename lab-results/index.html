Blood test explainer.

Lab reports are full of short names and two kinds of units. Paste your results, or type them in, and each test is shown in plain words: what it measures, where your number sits against a typical adult range, and what to ask your doctor. Values are shown in both mmol/L and mg/dL style units. Nothing leaves your browser.

This is an explainer, not medical advice. Labs use different methods and ranges, so the range printed on your own report is the one that counts. If the tool spots a range on a line it uses it, and you can edit any range below. A result just outside a range is often not a concern on its own. Talk to your doctor about what your results mean for you.

Why two units? Most of the world, and many UAE labs, report in SI units like mmol/L, which count molecules. US labs use mg/dL, which weigh them. Glucose of 5.5 mmol/L and 99 mg/dL are the same thing. The factor differs for each substance because each molecule has a different weight, which is why a single converter for "mmol to mg" doesn't exist.
