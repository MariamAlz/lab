Tonight's sunset.

Some evenings the whole sky turns pink and orange, others the sun just fades into grey. The difference is mostly cloud height, a clear gap on the western horizon, and how much dust is in the air. This scores tonight and the next three evenings from 0 to 100 and tells you when golden and blue hour start.

Why this score

    Why clouds help, but only the right ones. After the sun drops below your horizon, it still lights clouds kilometres above you from underneath. High and middle clouds (above about 2 km) catch that red light and glow. Low cloud near the western horizon does the opposite: it blocks the light before it reaches anything. That's why this checks the low cloud about 75 km out along the line to the sun, not just over your head.

    West-facing spots over water

      What's approximate. The score is a rule of thumb built from forecast cloud layers, humidity, visibility and modelled dust (Open-Meteo and CAMS). It isn't a measurement, and forecasts of thin high cloud are often wrong by an hour or two. Sun times and directions are computed in your browser to within about a minute and half a degree. The open-water directions for each spot are rough. The east coast (Fujairah, Khor Fakkan) faces the sunrise instead.
