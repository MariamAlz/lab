GPS interference now.

Satellite navigation signals reach the ground about as faint as a car headlight seen from 20,000 km away, so they're easy to drown out (jamming) or fake (spoofing). Aircraft constantly broadcast how much they trust their own position. When many planes in one area report poor accuracy at once, something on the ground is interfering. This map collects those reports live.

Each square is about 100 km across. A square needs at least 3 planes before it's coloured, and reports build up over 10 minutes so one faulty plane doesn't paint a whole area. Purple marks planes showing spoofing signs: a GPS height that disagrees with the barometric height, a position that jumps faster than any aircraft can fly, or a report that GPS was lost.

Check your own phone
  • ·Your phone reports its own accuracy and position. This watches them for a minute for signs of trouble.Works best outdoors with a clear view of the sky. Your location stays on the device.

Aircraft data comes from community ADS-B receiver networks (adsb.lol, airplanes.live, adsb.fi), fetched by your browser. Accuracy is read from the NIC and NACp values in each broadcast, the same method used by public interference maps. It shows where interference is likely, not who causes it, and gaps on the map are places with no planes or no receivers.
