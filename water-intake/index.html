Water in the heat.

"Eight glasses a day" ignores the weather. This works out a daily water target from your weight, exercise, where you work and today's heat and humidity where you are, and shows every step of the sum. Tap a glass each time you drink. In Ramadan it spreads your water between iftar and suhoor.

    Your body can only take in roughly 0.8 to 1 litre an hour, and drinking a lot at once mostly sends it straight to the bladder. Steady glasses through the evening work better than a big bottle at suhoor. Watery food counts too: soup, laban, watermelon and cucumber.

    How the target is worked out

      An estimate, not medical advice. If you have kidney or heart conditions, take water tablets, or have been told to limit fluids, follow your doctor's advice instead. Dizziness, confusion or no sweating in the heat is an emergency: get cool and call 998.

      Urine colour check
      1–3: well hydrated4–5: drink a glass soon6–8: drink now

      Colour is a quick, free hydration check that sports doctors use. Pale straw is the goal. Vitamin B supplements can turn urine bright yellow whatever you drink, and some foods and medicines change it too. Dark brown or red urine that doesn't clear with water needs a doctor.

      Why the weather matters

      Indoors in air conditioning you lose about the same water as anywhere else. The heat counts when you are out in it. Sweat rates in the Gulf summer can reach a litre an hour during physical work, so outdoor hours change the target far more than the thermometer alone. Humidity matters because sweat that can't evaporate doesn't cool you, so you keep sweating. That's why the sum uses the heat index (temperature plus humidity), not just the temperature.

      The starting point is about 30 ml per kilo of body weight from drinks, close to the European Food Safety Authority's adequate intake once food water is taken out. Pregnancy adds about 300 ml and breastfeeding about 700 ml. Exercise adds about 12 ml a minute, more in the heat. Outdoor work follows the usual occupational advice of about a cup every 15 to 20 minutes in high heat, capped well below the 1.4 litres an hour where drinking too much becomes a risk.
