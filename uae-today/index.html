Space · Earth · Live

The UAE from space, today.

Four NASA and NOAA satellites photograph the whole country every day, and the pictures are online within a few hours. Look for dust blowing in from the north-west, fog over the coast on winter mornings, and green or red patches in the sea. Pick a satellite and a day, then zoom in.

Satellite

What to look for · tap to zoom there
    About the pictures

    Morning or afternoon. Terra passes over the UAE in the morning, and Aqua, Suomi NPP and NOAA-20 in the early afternoon. Morning is better for fog, afternoon for dust that builds up as the ground heats.

    Why there are stripes or black wedges. Each satellite sees a strip about 2,300 to 3,000 km wide. Some days the UAE falls at the edge of a strip, where the view is stretched and hazy, or in a gap between two passes. Try another satellite for the same day.

    The "false colour" view uses infrared light that people can't see. Cloud made of ice turns bright cyan, vegetation turns green, bare desert is pinkish brown and dust stays a soft beige. It helps tell high cloud from a dust plume when both look white in true colour.

    Imagery from NASA GIBS and Worldview: MODIS on Terra and Aqua, VIIRS on Suomi NPP and NOAA-20. Each pixel is about 250 to 500 metres, so you'll see islands and cities, not buildings. Place names © OpenStreetMap contributors, © CARTO.
