Letter to the future.

Write a letter to your child for their 18th birthday, to yourself in five years, or to a friend for next Eid. It gets locked so that nobody can read it before the date you pick. Not you, not the person you send it to, and not this website. The letter travels inside the link itself.

How the lock works
  1. A public clock that signs. drand is run by a group of independent organisations, the League of Entropy, including Cloudflare, Protocol Labs and several universities. Together they publish a new signed random number every 3 seconds, numbered in order. Round 1 was in August 2023, so every future date has a known round number.
  2. Locked to a signature that doesn't exist yet. Your letter is encrypted in your browser so that the only thing that can unlock it is the network's signature for the round at your chosen date. That signature is made at that moment, by most members working together. Before then nobody has it, so nobody can open the letter.
  3. No key to lose or leak. There is no password and no copy on a server. The scrambled letter sits inside the link after the # sign, which browsers never send to websites. When the date arrives, the page fetches that round's public signature and uses it to unlock the letter.

Honest limits: if the drand network shuts down before your date, the letter can never be opened. If most of its members secretly worked together, they could open it early. Keep the link or file somewhere safe, because it is the only copy. The lock uses the open tlock format with the drand "quicknet" chain, so other tlock tools can open it too.
