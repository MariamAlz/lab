How the ranking works

Dark hours count the time when the Sun is more than 18° below the horizon (astronomical night) and the Moon is down or only a thin sliver. That's when the faint stars and the Milky Way show. Each night's score is those dark hours, reduced by the forecast cloud cover, with a penalty for strong wind and a bonus for hours when the Milky Way's core is up.

The Milky Way core is the bright, dusty centre of our galaxy, in Sagittarius. From the UAE it's best from March to September. In winter it's below the horizon all night, but the winter sky has Orion, Sirius and the Pleiades instead.

Moon times are calculated for the spot. Weather comes from the Open-Meteo forecast, which covers up to 16 days but becomes much less reliable after 4 or 5. Forecasts can't see local dust, so check again on the day.

Before you go

Let your eyes adjust for 20 minutes with no phone screens, or use a red light. Park and camp well away from car headlights and the glow on the horizon from cities. Desert nights in winter can drop below 10°C, and Jebel Jais can get close to freezing. Wind over 30 km/h lifts sand and makes tents miserable.

Tell someone where you're going, carry plenty of water and a charged phone, and only drive in the dunes with experience and a second car. Some areas are protected or need permission for camping, so check local rules first.
