What your photo gives away.

Most photos carry hidden notes from the camera: where you stood, when, which phone or camera took it, sometimes its serial number and your name. Some even keep a tiny preview of the picture from before you cropped it. Drop a photo to see what's inside, then save a clean copy before you share it.

Before you share a photo
  • Big platforms such as Instagram, X and Facebook remove location when they publish, but they still receive it. Email, WhatsApp "document" mode, AirDrop, Telegram files, cloud links and forums usually send the original untouched.
  • On iPhone, tap Options on the share sheet and switch off Location. On Android, look for "Remove location data" in the share or camera settings.
  • To stop it at the source, turn off location tags in the camera app's settings.
  • Cropping does not always remove the cropped part. Some editors leave the old preview inside the file. Clean the file after cropping.
  • Screenshots carry less metadata, but they can show your status bar, battery, carrier and time.

How cleaning works. The photo is drawn onto a blank canvas and saved again. A canvas only holds pixels, so every hidden block (EXIF, GPS, XMP, IPTC, maker notes, the embedded preview) is left behind. The catch is that a JPEG is compressed a second time, so the clean copy can be slightly softer.

The hidden preview. JPEG files from cameras and phones usually include a 160-pixel thumbnail so galleries load fast. Older editing tools updated the main picture but not the thumbnail. People have shared cropped photos where the thumbnail still showed the part they cut out.
