When to sell your car.

Cars lose value on a curve, with a few cliffs along the way: the end of the warranty, 100,000 km, the five-year mark and the new model arriving. Enter your car and this shows where you are on its curve, what the next cliff costs, and whether keeping it or switching is cheaper per kilometre.

    These are rough estimates from typical UAE resale patterns, not a valuation. Real prices swing with condition, colour, service history, accident record and demand. Get two or three offers before you decide.

    Why GCC spec matters. Cars built for the Gulf have bigger radiators, stronger AC and dust filtering, and dealers here can service them under warranty. Imported US or Japanese spec cars often carry hidden accident or flood history, so buyers pay noticeably less for them.

    How the curve works. A new car drops most in its first year, then loses a roughly steady share of its remaining value each year: an exponential decay. Each cliff is a step in what buyers will pay, smoothed over a few months. Mileage moves the whole curve: each 10,000 km above about 20,000 a year knocks off around 2%.
