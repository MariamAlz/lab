How unique is your browser?

Websites can recognise you without cookies. Your screen size, fonts, graphics card, time zone and dozens of other small details add up to a fingerprint that often picks you out of millions. Here is exactly what any site can read right now, how much each detail gives away, and how to blend in.

    How to blend in

    • Blend in, don't stand out. Extensions that fake your user agent or block one API often make you rarer, not safer.
    • Fewer extras. Unusual fonts, many extensions and odd window sizes all add bits.
    • Separate lives. Use one browser for banking and accounts and another for casual browsing, so a fingerprint in one doesn't link to the other.
    • Cookies still matter. Fingerprinting is one tool. Clearing cookies and blocking third-party trackers removes the easier ones.

    How the score works. Information is measured in bits: each bit halves the crowd you could belong to. About 33 bits is enough to single out one person among everyone on Earth. The bits per detail are approximations based on published studies of millions of browsers (EFF's Panopticlick, AmIUnique and later research), adjusted for how common your particular value is. Adding them up assumes the details are independent, which they aren't (a Mac user agent comes with Mac fonts), so the total is cut by a quarter to allow for overlap. Treat it as a rough guide, not a measurement.

    Canvas and audio fingerprints work because every combination of graphics chip, driver, font renderer and operating system draws pixels and processes sound in very slightly different ways. The page draws a hidden image and generates a silent tone, then hashes the result. You never see it, and neither would you on a site that does this quietly.
