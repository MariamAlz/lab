Medicine timing in Ramadan.

When you fast, every dose has to fit between iftar and suhoor. Add your medicines and this lays them out across the night using today's prayer times for your city. Use it as a starting point for a conversation with your doctor or pharmacist, not as instructions.

Talk to your doctor or pharmacist first.

Ideally do it a few weeks before Ramadan. They can tell you if a dose can move, if there's a once-daily version, or if fasting is safe for you at all. This page only suggests clock times. It never changes how much you take, and it can't know your full medical picture.

      Safety rules
      • Never change insulin, diabetes tablets, blood thinners, epilepsy medicines or any other critical medicine on your own. Moving them can cause low blood sugar, clots, bleeding or seizures. Your doctor has to plan it.
      • Don't skip doses or double up to "catch up" after the fast.
      • Medicines taken 3 or 4 times a day are the hardest to fit. Often a doctor can switch to a long-acting version taken once or twice.
      • If you feel dizzy, shaky, confused, faint or very unwell while fasting, break your fast and get medical help. Call 999 in an emergency.

      Islam does not ask people to fast when it harms their health. Many scholars point to the verse on fasting (Al-Baqarah 2:184) that excuses those who are ill, who can make up the days later or give fidyah if they can't. Whether inhalers, injections, eye drops and similar treatments break the fast is a question scholars answer differently. Ask a scholar you trust, together with your doctor. This page doesn't give a ruling.

      How the times are chosen

      Once a day goes with iftar, or before suhoor if it should be taken on an empty stomach. Twice a day goes at iftar and suhoor. Three times adds a dose around the middle of the night, but the gaps can't be even: the long gap across the fasting day is usually 14 hours or more. Four times doesn't fit safely in one night, so it's flagged for your doctor instead.

      Prayer times use the UAE method (Fajr when the sun is 18.2° below the horizon, Maghrib at sunset). They are calculated, so they can differ by a minute or two from your mosque or the official timetable. Ramadan dates use the Umm al-Qura calendar in your browser; in the UAE the start is confirmed by moon sighting. Your medicine list stays in this browser only.
