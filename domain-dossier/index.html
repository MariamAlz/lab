Domain dossier.

One page with everything public about a domain: when it was registered and by which registrar, its DNS records, how well it protects its email from spoofing, every certificate and subdomain in public logs, who hosts it, and which look-alike names exist. Use it to check a suspicious sender, or to audit your own domain the way an attacker would.

Look-alike domains generated on your device

Why age matters. Most phishing domains are registered days before an attack and abandoned within weeks. A domain under 30 days old that asks you to log in or pay is a strong warning sign. Old domains can still be hijacked or resold, so age is a clue, not proof.

What gets sent where. Only the domain name leaves your device: to rdap.org (which forwards to the right registry), to Cloudflare or Google for DNS lookups, to certspotter and crt.sh for certificate logs, and the domain's IP address to ipwho.is for the network owner. Look-alikes are generated here; checking them sends up to 40 more DNS lookups. Nothing is stored.
