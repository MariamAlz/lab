Home energy audit.

An electricity bill gives you one number. This splits it up. List what you have in each room and roughly how many hours it runs, and you'll see the kWh and dirhams each one costs a month on your tariff. Then you get the savings ranked by how much they would save in your home. A sample two-bedroom flat is filled in to start.

      How it is worked out

      Each line is watts × number × hours a day × on % × 30.4 days ÷ 1000 = kWh a month. The bill applies each slab in turn, so the last kWh you use costs the most. That is why savings are worked out by re-running your whole bill without them, not by a flat rate.

      In UAE homes, cooling is often 60 to 70% of the summer bill. A ton of AC is 12,000 BTU an hour of cooling. An older split unit draws around 1.4 kW per ton when the compressor runs, a newer inverter unit closer to 0.8. If your building has district cooling (Empower, Emicool, Tabreed and others), your AC is billed separately, so remove AC lines here and compare against your electricity bill only.

      These are estimates. Real use depends on insulation, sun on your windows, thermostat settings and the age of each machine. Check your actual bill and the DEWA, ADDC, AADC, SEWA or EtihadWE app for your real readings. The municipality housing fee and water are not included.
