Rain radar, UAE.

Rain in the UAE is rare, but when it comes it can be heavy and fast. This plays back the last two hours of weather radar so you can see where the storms are and which way they are moving. Press play, or drag the slider to step through time.

What the colours mean
LightDrizzle, light shower
ModerateSteady rain, wet roads
HeavyPuddles form fast
Very heavyThunderstorm, flooding likely
ExtremeHail possible

Radar sends out radio pulses and listens for echoes off raindrops. Bigger and more numerous drops send back more energy, measured in dBZ. Each step of 10 dBZ is ten times more echo, which is why the scale jumps so quickly from drizzle to downpour. The colours are approximate. Radar can also show false echoes near mountains and the coast, and dust in the air can create faint speckles on a clear day.

Watch the last few frames to see which way a storm is drifting. Storms over the Hajar mountains in summer afternoons often build quickly and stay over the same valleys for an hour or more.

Wadis and flooded roads
  • Stay out of wadis when rain is anywhere upstream. A storm over the mountains can send a flash flood down a dry valley where the sky above you is still blue.
  • Don't drive into moving water. About 30 cm of flowing water can float a car. Underpasses fill first.
  • Park on high ground and away from wadi beds when camping or hiking in Hatta, Ras Al Khaimah or Fujairah.
  • Follow official warnings. The National Center of Meteorology issues yellow, orange and red alerts, and police may close roads and wadis. Those come first, not this map.
  • In an emergency call 999.
