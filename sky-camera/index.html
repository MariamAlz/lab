Space · Camera · Live

Sky through your camera.

Hold your phone up to the night sky and the stars, planets, Moon and UAE satellites are labelled right over the camera view, with the old Arabic star names many of them still carry. Without a phone, drag the sky to look around.

Your phone will ask for the camera, motion sensors and location. Nothing leaves your device.

Names

If labels sit off their stars, match the field of view to your camera and wave the phone in a figure of eight to settle the compass. Phone compasses are thrown off by cars, metal railings and magnetic cases.

What you're seeing

Positions are worked out on your phone from the time and your location: around 100 of the brightest stars, the Sun, Moon, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn, and the UAE's satellites from fresh orbit data. Many star names in use worldwide are Arabic: Aldebaran (الدبران, "the follower"), Betelgeuse (يد الجوزاء), Altair (الطائر) and Vega (الواقع).

Most UAE satellites are in geostationary orbit, about 36,000 km up. They sit still over the equator to the south and are far too faint to see by eye. MBZ-SAT and other low satellites move across the sky in a few minutes.
