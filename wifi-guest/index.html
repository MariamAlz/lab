Wi-Fi guest card.

Stop reading out your Wi-Fi password letter by letter. Make a card with a QR code that guests scan to join, print it for the majlis or the fridge door, and give guests their own network while you're at it.

Your password never leaves this page. The QR code is drawn in your browser, nothing is uploaded, and the password is not saved.

    Guest password generator

    Easy-to-type passwords skip characters people mix up, like 0 and O or 1, l and I, and use groups of four so a guest can type it on a TV remote.

    Card design

    Preview. Printing fits the card to one A6 or A5 sheet. In the print dialog, turn off headers and footers.

    Why guests get their own network

    Anyone on your main Wi-Fi can see the other devices on it: your laptop, shared folders, printer and cameras. A guest's phone that picked up malware elsewhere can poke at them too. A guest network gives visitors the internet and nothing else, and you can change its password after a big gathering without reconnecting all your own devices.

    Smart plugs, TVs, cameras and doorbells are a good fit for a separate network too. They rarely get security updates, and if one is broken into it can't reach your phone or laptop. Many smart devices only work on 2.4 GHz, so keep that band switched on for that network.

    Setting up a guest network at home

    Most home routers from e& and du have a Guest Wi-Fi option in the router's admin page. The exact menus differ between router models, so use these as general steps:

    1. Find the admin page address and admin password on the sticker under the router.
    2. Open that address in a browser while connected to your Wi-Fi and sign in.
    3. Look in the wireless or Wi-Fi settings for Guest Wi-Fi or Guest network, and turn it on.
    4. Give it a name, choose WPA2 or WPA3, and set a password made here.
    5. If there's an option to keep guests away from your local network, often called isolation, turn it on.
    6. Save, then make the card above with the same name and password.

    If you can't find the option or the admin password, your provider's app or support line can help. Change the router's admin password from the default while you're there.
