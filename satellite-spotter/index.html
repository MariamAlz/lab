ISS & satellite spotter.

The International Space Station is the brightest thing in the night sky after the Moon and Venus. It's easy to see from the UAE if you know when and where to look. It shows up just after sunset or before dawn, when you're in darkness but it's still lit by the Sun. Here are the next passes over you, and an arrow that points your phone straight at it.

    Orbits come from CelesTrak and are refreshed every few hours. The ISS fires its engines now and then, which can shift times by a minute, so check again on the day. A pass is listed only when the satellite rises at least 10° and is sunlit while your sky is dark. Brightness uses the magnitude scale, where lower is brighter: Venus is about −4 and the faintest stars you can see in a city are about +3.
