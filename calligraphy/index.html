Arabic letter tracing.

Trace each of the 28 Arabic letters over a faint guide, then try a few joined words. Every attempt gets a score for shape, stroke direction and stroke order, with a tip on what to fix. It works with a finger, a stylus or a mouse, and your best scores stay on this device.

How the score works. Your line and the guide are each resampled to 48 evenly spaced points, and the score comes from the average gap between matching points. It also checks the same comparison backwards, so it can tell when a stroke was drawn the wrong way, and it checks whether the strokes came in the right order. Dots can be a tap or a short dash.

Why the dot matters. Classical Naskh is measured in dots: the small rhombus a reed pen leaves when pressed once. The 10th-century Baghdad vizier Ibn Muqla is credited with a system that sized each letter in these dots and in proportion to the alif. The guides here are simplified, Naskh-like shapes for practice, not a calligrapher's exemplar.
