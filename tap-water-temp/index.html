Hot "cold" water.

From June to September the cold tap in many UAE homes runs warmer than the hot one. That's because the water sits in a tank on the roof, cooking in the sun all day. This forecasts how hot your cold tap will be today and tomorrow, and the best time for a cool shower.

Why it happens

Most villas and many buildings store water in a plastic or fibreglass tank on the roof. In summer the air stays above 30°C even at night, so the water never cools much. During the day the sun adds hundreds of watts to every tank. A thousand litres takes a long time to heat up or cool down, so the water ends up a few degrees above the day's average air temperature, peaking in the late afternoon. The pipes running across a hot roof and down sunny walls add a little more.

How it works. The tank is one lump of water. Each 10 minutes it gains sunlight (more for a dark tank, less in shade), swaps heat with the air through its walls (slower with an insulating jacket), loses a little to the clear night sky, and mixes in fresh mains water as you use it. It warms up on three days of past weather before forecasting. Real tanks vary, so expect it to be within a few degrees.

Ways to keep it cooler
  • Shade the tank. A simple canopy or shade cloth over it cuts the sun's heat by about three quarters.
  • An insulating jacket slows heat coming in from the hot air and sun.
  • White or light-coloured tanks absorb far less sunlight than dark blue or black ones.
  • Shower in the early morning, when the tank is at its coolest.
  • Fill buckets or a bath at night or before sunrise and let them sit indoors.
  • Run the cold tap for a minute. The first water out has been sitting in pipes on the roof.
  • A tank water cooler (a chiller unit on the roof) is the only way to get properly cold water in August. Ask a licensed plumber.

Above about 50°C, water can scald, especially for children and older people. If your "cold" water ever gets that hot, test it before a bath. Weather from Open-Meteo.
