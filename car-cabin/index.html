Car cabin melt timer.

"I'll only be ten minutes." A parked car in the sun gets about 20°C hotter than outside within the hour, and most of that happens in the first half hour. This estimates how hot your car will get from the live weather where you are, and how long things left inside have before they're at risk.

Children and pets: never.

Never leave a child or an animal in a parked car, not for one minute, not with the windows open, not in the shade. If you see one alone in a car, call 999.

Keeping things safe
  • Take your phone, laptop and power bank with you. The glovebox and boot get hot too, just a little more slowly.
  • Never charge a phone or power bank in a hot parked car. If one is swollen, smells or is hot to touch, leave it to cool away from the car and don't use it.
  • If your phone shows a temperature warning, turn it off, move it to the shade or air conditioning, and let it cool on its own. Don't put it in the fridge.
  • Carry insulin and other medicines in a cool bag, and check the leaflet for its storage temperature.
  • Make the supermarket your last stop, and keep a cool bag in the boot for dairy, meat and eggs.
  • Don't leave water bottles to bake in the car for days. Use a fresh one.
  • Lighters, aerosol cans and perfume can leak or burst in extreme heat. Keep them out of the car in summer.

How it works. Sunlight passes through the glass and warms the seats and dashboard, which then heat the air. That heat can't get back out through the glass, the same greenhouse effect as a glasshouse. Studies of parked cars (McLaren and colleagues, 2005; Vanos and colleagues, 2018) found the cabin rises by about 20 to 25°C over the outside air in an hour of full sun, with around 80% of that in the first 30 minutes. Cracking a window made little difference.

This page models the cabin as warming towards outside air plus a sun boost, with a 19-minute time constant. The boost scales with live sunshine and is cut to about a third in shade, by 15% for tinted windows and 20% for a sunshade. Items start at about 25°C, as if just taken from an air-conditioned car or home, and each one warms more slowly than the air around it. Limits are typical: check your device or medicine's own guidance. It's an estimate, not medical or safety advice. Weather from Open-Meteo.
