Your pulse from a fingertip.

Rest a fingertip over your phone's rear camera and torch. Each heartbeat pushes a little more blood into your finger, which makes it slightly darker red. The camera sees that tiny change about once a second, and this page turns it into a waveform and a heart rate.

A demo, not a medical device. The number is an estimate and can be wrong, especially with cold hands, movement or a weak torch. If you feel unwell, chest pain, or a racing or irregular heartbeat, talk to a doctor or call 998.

How to get a good reading
  1. Sit down and rest for a minute first.
  2. Rest your fingertip lightly over the camera and the torch. Don't press hard: squeezing pushes the blood out.
  3. Keep your hand and phone still, resting on a table if you can.
  4. Wait about 20 seconds for a steady result.
Your readings (this device only)

    This is photoplethysmography (PPG), the same idea as the clip a nurse puts on your finger and the green lights under a smartwatch. Light goes into the skin and some bounces back. Blood absorbs light, so with every beat a little less comes back. The change is under 1% of the brightness, so the page averages every pixel's red value, removes slow drift, and keeps only the rhythms between 40 and 200 beats a minute. It then finds the repeat time of the wave (autocorrelation) and checks it against the gaps between individual peaks.

    A typical resting heart rate for adults is 60 to 100 beats a minute. Fit people and athletes are often lower. Heat, caffeine, stress and dehydration all raise it, which is worth knowing in a UAE summer.
