Laundry dry time.

In a UAE summer a t-shirt can dry on the balcony faster than the next load washes. On a humid night it can sit damp until morning, and a dusty afternoon can undo the wash. This uses the live forecast for your spot to tell you how long it will take, the best hour to hang it today, and when to bring it in.

How it works. Drying is evaporation, and two things drive it. The first is how thirsty the air is: the vapour pressure deficit, or how much more water the air could hold. Hot dry air in June can take four times as much as a humid August evening on the coast. The second is energy: sunlight on the cloth supplies the heat that turns water into vapour, and wind carries the damp air away. This page combines them with the Penman equation, the same one farmers use to estimate how fast fields lose water.

The fabric numbers are typical: how much water each item holds after a normal spin, its hanging area, and a slow-down for thick cloth whose inside dries last. Indoors assumes an air-conditioned room at about 24°C and 50% humidity with little air flow. Your machine, your balcony and your towels will differ, so treat the times as a guide.
