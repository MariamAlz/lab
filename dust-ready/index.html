What is dust AOD?

Aerosol optical depth measures how much the whole column of air above you dims sunlight, from the ground to the top of the sky. It has no units. Clear desert air is around 0.1 to 0.3. During a big shamal it can pass 2, when only a few percent of direct sunlight gets through and the sun looks like a pale disc.

AOD and ground-level dust don't always agree. A dust layer can sit a kilometre up, making the sky milky while the air you breathe is fine. That's why this page looks at PM10 (particles under 10 micrometres at breathing height) and visibility too. The level for each hour takes the worst of them.

Satellites measure AOD from space, and you can see dust plumes crossing the Gulf on UAE today, the lab's daily satellite view.

Forecasts are from Open-Meteo (dust from the CAMS model). They are models, not readings, and dust storms can arrive faster than forecast. Follow the National Center of Meteorology and police alerts. This is general guidance, not medical advice.
