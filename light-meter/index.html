Is there enough light here?

Our eyes adjust so well that a dim room and a bright one can feel the same, even when one has ten times more light. This uses your phone camera to estimate the light level in lux, then tells you whether it suits reading, desk work or a screen, and which houseplants would be happy there.

Is it right for…

    An estimate. Phone cameras aren't built to measure light, and the number can be off by a factor of two or more. It's good for comparing spots (this corner versus that window) and for a rough idea. For eye strain that doesn't go away, check with an optometrist.

    Houseplants by light
    Low light · 50 to 2,500 luxSnake plant (sansevieria), ZZ plant, pothos (money plant), peace lily, cast-iron plant, Chinese evergreen (aglaonema), heartleaf philodendron. Fine a few metres from a window or under office lights.
    Medium light · 2,500 to 10,000 luxMonstera, rubber plant, spider plant, calathea, dracaena, Boston fern, areca palm. Near a bright window but out of direct sun.
    Bright light · 10,000 lux and upCacti and succulents, aloe vera, desert rose (adenium), jade plant, fiddle-leaf fig, herbs like basil and mint, bougainvillea. A sunny window or a balcony. In a UAE summer, even these like shade at midday.

    Lux measures how much light lands on a surface. A moonlit night gives under 1 lux, a living room 50 to 150, an office 300 to 500, an overcast day around 2,000 to 10,000, and direct UAE sun over 100,000. Indoors near a window, light drops quickly: a metre or two back can mean a tenth of the light, even though it looks almost as bright to you.

    How it works. A camera chooses a shutter speed and sensitivity (ISO) to make the picture look normal. When the browser shares those settings, the page can work backwards: a bright picture taken with a fast shutter and low ISO means a lot of light. A white sheet of paper reflects about 80% of the light that hits it, which turns brightness into lux. When the settings are hidden, a one-time calibration against a known spot does the same job.
