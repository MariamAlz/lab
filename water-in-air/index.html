How much water is in the air.

On a humid UAE summer night, the outdoor air that would fill an ordinary bedroom carries about a litre of water as invisible vapour. This turns the live temperature and humidity at your spot into numbers you can picture: grams per cubic metre, litres in your room, what your AC drips out each hour, and whether tomorrow morning will be foggy.

Wet car and sweating windows

    Tips: a windscreen wet on the outside needs wipers, not cold air. Blowing AC onto it chills the glass and makes it worse, so point the vents away or set the screen to warm. Misting on the inside (cool, damp winter mornings) is the opposite: AC on the windscreen setting dries the air and clears it. Before stepping out with glasses or a camera, let them warm up in a pocket or bag for a few minutes.

    Why mornings get foggy. The dew point is the temperature at which the air is full and can't hold its water as vapour any more. On clear, calm nights the ground loses heat to the sky and the air above it cools. If it cools down to the dew point, the extra water condenses on dust and salt particles as tiny droplets: fog. Coastal UAE gets it most from September to March, when nights are cool and sea air is moist. It usually burns off within a couple of hours of sunrise.

    Why glasses and car windows steam up. Any surface colder than the dew point collects water. Step out of a 22°C car into air with a 28°C dew point and your sunglasses fog instantly. In winter it flips: your breath makes the air inside the car more humid than the cold windscreen can handle, so it mists on the inside. Point the AC at the glass: it dries the air first.

    Your room

    A cheap hygrometer will tell you the room's humidity. Most AC'd UAE homes sit at 40–60%.

    Your AC

    Typical figures, edit to match yours. Split units move about 400 cubic feet a minute per ton (680 m³/h per ton). The cold coil inside the unit is usually 8 to 14°C. Picking a size fills in a typical airflow.
