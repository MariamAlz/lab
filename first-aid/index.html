Home first-aid kit check.

Most home kits are a box of old plasters and a painkiller that expired two summers ago. This page gives you a kit list for your household and the UAE climate, tracks what you have and when it expires, and turns the gaps into a shopping list. There is also a quick guide for common emergencies, to read now rather than during one.

    Why expiry matters more here: medicines are tested for storage at up to 25 or 30°C. A car parked in a UAE summer can pass 60°C inside, and storerooms and balconies often sit above 40°C. Heat breaks down tablets, melts creams and dries out burn gel faster than the printed date assumes.

    Add a medicine
    UAE summer heat: inside a parked car, a storeroom without AC, or on a balcony, temperatures go well above 25°C, and above 30°C most of the year. Keep medicines in an air-conditioned room, away from the stove, the window and the bathroom steam. Insulin and other fridge medicines need a cool bag for travel.
    Your cabinet

      Take expired or heat-damaged medicines to a pharmacy for safe disposal instead of the bin. Ask the pharmacist if you are unsure whether something is still fine.

      This is not medical training. It is a short summary of standard first-aid guidance of the kind taught by the Red Crescent and Red Cross. In an emergency call 998 for an ambulance (999 for police) and follow the dispatcher. A real first-aid course, with hands-on CPR practice, is worth a few hours of anyone's time.

      Kit lists follow common Red Cross and Red Crescent home-kit suggestions, adapted for heat. Doses are not given on purpose: always follow the pack or your pharmacist, especially for children. Your list stays in this browser only.
