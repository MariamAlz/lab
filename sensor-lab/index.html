Your phone's hidden lab.

Your phone carries an accelerometer and a gyroscope that most apps use quietly for screen rotation and step counts. Here you can see them live and use them for real experiments: count the floors a lift climbs, measure gravity with a pendulum, check a shelf is level, and more. Open it on your phone.

How many floors did the lift go?

  1. Get in the lift and hold the phone still (or put it flat on the floor).
  2. Press Start while the doors are still open. Keep still for 2 seconds while it calibrates.
  3. Ride the lift. Press Stop when the doors open.
0.0m

Floors travelled appear here.

The lift only pushes you during the first and last two or three seconds of a ride. In between it moves at a steady speed and you feel nothing. Adding up the acceleration once gives speed, and adding up speed gives height. Small errors grow fast, so the page assumes the lift ends at rest and corrects for that.

Measure gravity with a pendulum.

  1. Hang the phone from a charger cable or string, screen facing you. Something soft below, please.
  2. Measure from the pivot point to the middle of the phone and type it in.
  3. Pull it a little to one side (small swings), let go, then press Start. It times 10 swings.
  1. Hang any small weight (keys work) from a string and measure its length to the weight's centre.
  2. Pull it a little to one side and let go.
  3. Tap the big button every time the weight passes its lowest point going right. Ten taps or more is good.

Period: --

A pendulum's swing time depends only on its length and gravity: T = 2π√(L/g). So timing the swing measures g. In the UAE it's about 9.79 m/s², slightly less than at the poles (9.83) because the Earth bulges at the equator and spins. A phone is not a point weight, so expect to land within a few percent.

Spirit level and shelf check.

--°

Lay the phone on the surface.

A phone's camera bump can tilt it, so calibrate on a surface you trust. Or try the carpenter's trick: read the angle, turn the phone round 180° on the same spot, and read again. The real slope is the average of the two. On a 1 m shelf, 0.5° is about 9 mm from one end to the other.

Step counter.

  1. Put the phone in a pocket or hold it in your hand.
  2. Press Start and walk normally.
0steps

Cadence and distance appear here.

Every step sends a small jolt through your body, about 1.2 to 1.5 times gravity at heel strike. The counter smooths the signal and counts the jolts that are at least a third of a second apart. Stride length is roughly 41% of your height.

How hard was that?

  1. Shake the phone, tap it on your palm, or drop it onto a bed or cushion from a low height.
  2. Please don't drop it onto anything hard.
1.00g peak

The strongest jolt since you last reset.

While it's falling, the phone feels weightless, so its accelerometer reads close to zero. The page times that quiet moment and works out the drop height from h = ½gt². A hard landing on a tiled floor can reach several hundred g for a millisecond, but phones only sample about 60 to 100 times a second, so short spikes are under-read.

The accelerometer is a tiny silicon comb on a spring. When the phone accelerates, the comb lags behind and the gap between its teeth changes, which changes a capacitance. Lying still on a table, it reads 9.8 m/s² upward: that's the table pushing back against gravity. The gyroscope uses a vibrating mass that gets nudged sideways when the phone turns.
