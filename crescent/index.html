Will the crescent be seen tonight?

Each Hijri month starts with the first thin crescent after the new moon. Whether it can be seen depends on how far it is from the Sun, how long it stays up after sunset, and how thick it is. This works out those numbers for the evenings around each new moon and applies a published visibility rule, so you know which evening is worth going out to look.

What the numbers mean
Age
Hours since the new moon (conjunction), when the Moon passes the Sun. A young Moon is thin and close to the Sun.
Lag
Minutes between sunset and moonset. More time means a darker sky while the Moon is still up.
ARCV
How much higher the Moon is than the Sun, in degrees.
ARCL
The angle between the Moon and the Sun (elongation). Below about 7° no crescent forms that anyone has seen.
W
The crescent's width in arcminutes. Even a good crescent is only about 0.5′ thick, less than a sixtieth of the Moon's width.

The verdict uses Odeh's criterion (2004), fitted to 737 real sighting reports. It compares ARCV with the width W at the "best time", which is sunset plus four ninths of the lag: V = ARCV − (−0.1018W³ + 0.7319W² − 6.3226W + 7.1651). V ≥ 5.65 means naked eye, 2 to 5.65 optical aid and maybe naked eye, −0.96 to 2 optical aid only, below −0.96 not visible. The older Yallop q value is shown for comparison.

Calculation vs sighting

A calculation can say whether a crescent is possible to see. It can't promise someone will see it. Dust, haze and city lights near the horizon, the observer's eyesight and simply knowing where to look all matter. In the UAE, summer haze often hides crescents that the numbers say should be visible.

That's why months are announced in different ways. Many authorities, including in the UAE, still wait for a confirmed sighting by a moon-sighting committee, and use calculations to judge whether a reported sighting is believable. The Umm al-Qura calendar used for labels on this page is calculated in advance: a month starts when the Moon sets after the Sun in Makkah on the evening after the new moon, whether or not a crescent can be seen.

How it's calculated

Sun and Moon positions use a low-precision orbit model (good to a couple of arcminutes), corrected for where you stand on Earth: the Moon is close enough that it looks about one degree lower from the ground than from Earth's centre. Sunset and moonset include normal refraction. Altitudes in the criteria are "airless", as the criteria require. Times are UAE time (Makkah time for Makkah, your device's time zone for your own location).

This is an estimate for planning, not a ruling. For the official start of a month, follow the announcement from the relevant authority.
