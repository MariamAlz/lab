Qibla and the compass, explained.

Point your phone and turn until the gold arrow lines up with the top. This shows the direction of the Kaaba from where you are, how far it is, and the small gap between true north and magnetic north. On a laptop it still shows the bearing on a compass card, so you can line it up with a landmark.

Why the arrow points there

The Qibla is the direction of the shortest path over the globe to the Kaaba, a great circle. It's worked out with spherical trigonometry from your latitude and longitude. From the UAE it points a little south of west. From New York it points north-east, not south-east, because the shortest route curves up over the Atlantic.

This map is centred on you. On this projection every straight line from the centre is a great circle, so the Qibla is simply a straight line. Distances from the centre are true.

True north vs magnetic north

A compass needle points to magnetic north, which is not the North Pole. The difference is called declination. It changes from place to place and slowly over the years as Earth's molten core moves. Here it's worked out with the World Magnetic Model 2025, the same model phones and navigation systems use. In the UAE it's only about 2° east, so it barely matters for prayer. In parts of Canada or Russia it can be 20° or more.

Most phones correct for declination themselves once they know your location. Android's absolute orientation and iPhone's compass heading may or may not, depending on the device. If your phone's own compass app shows true north, tick the box under the compass.

Why phones get it wrong

Calibrate. Phone compasses drift. Wave the phone in a slow figure of eight a few times, away from anything metal. Metal and magnets nearby, like steel desks, car bodies, reinforced concrete, speakers or a magnetic phone case, can bend the reading by 10° to 30°. Hold it flat or upright and steady, and step away from walls and cars.

A good check without any sensor: at the right time of day the Sun is directly above Makkah (about 12:18 on 28 May and 12:27 on 16 July, Makkah time), and shadows everywhere that can see the Sun point straight away from the Qibla. Mosques also show the direction, so you can compare.

This is a helper, not a religious ruling. If in doubt, follow the direction of your local mosque.
