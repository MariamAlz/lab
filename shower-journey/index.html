Your shower's journey.

Almost every drop from a UAE tap started as Gulf seawater. Tell it how your household uses water and it follows that water from the sea, through a desalination plant, into your shower and back out as treated water for the city's trees. Along the way it adds up the seawater, energy, CO₂ and salty brine behind it.

    Where the numbers come from

    All figures are rough averages, meant for a sense of scale. Real plants vary with season, salinity and age, and your own supply may blend water from several plants. The Gulf is saltier and warmer than most seas, which pushes energy use up and recovery down compared with plants elsewhere.

    • Recovery ratios (reverse osmosis 0.42, multi-stage flash 0.22) and the UAE's share of world brine (about 20%): Jones et al., The state of desalination and brine production, Science of the Total Environment, 2019.
    • Thermal (MSF) plants use about 2.5–3.5 kWh of electricity plus around 80 kWh of heat per m³; reverse osmosis 3.5–5 kWh: IRENA–ETSAP Water Desalination brief. Masdar's UAE pilots reached under 3.6 kWh/m³ with reverse osmosis: Masdar desalination factsheet, which also notes Abu Dhabi gets virtually all its drinking water from desalination.
    • Dubai's capacity mix (427 MIGD thermal, 63 MIGD reverse osmosis in 2023, with all new capacity to 2030 being reverse osmosis): DEWA results, Q3 2023.
    • Grid emissions about 0.40 kg CO₂ per kWh: DEWA sustainability report 2021, via country grid emission factors.
    • Treated wastewater reuse in Abu Dhabi around half, with pipelines planned to reach 70%: Gulf News.
    • Pumping, distribution and sewage treatment energy (about 0.4 and 0.6 kWh/m³) and the heat-to-electricity equivalent for thermal plants are typical engineering values, not UAE-specific measurements. Change them above if you have better numbers.
