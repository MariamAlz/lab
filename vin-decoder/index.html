VIN decoder.

Every car since 1981 has a 17-character VIN, also called the chassis number. It's printed on the mulkiya, at the bottom of the windscreen and inside the driver's door. It tells you who made the car, where it was built and its model year, and whether the number has been typed or altered wrongly. Check it before you buy a used car, and compare it with the papers.

GCC spec or import?

A GCC-spec car was built for this region: a bigger radiator and stronger AC, dust filters, and a warranty and parts with local dealers. Imports from the US, Canada or Japan are often cheaper, but some arrive after an insurance write-off, a flood or a big crash. They can be fine. They just need more checking, and resale is usually lower.

The VIN shows where a car was built, not where it was sold. A Japan-built Land Cruiser starting with J can be GCC spec, and a US-built Tahoe starting with 1 can be too. So use the VIN with other clues: a speedometer in miles first, US-style amber side markers, a US radio band, a dealer service history abroad, or papers saying "imported". Cars made for the Japanese home market often carry a short frame number (like GRS200-1234567) instead of a 17-character VIN.

Before you buy a used car in the UAE
  • Match the VIN in three placesMulkiya, windscreen plate and door pillar sticker. Scratched or replaced plates are a warning sign.
  • Check the official historyAs of 2026, Dubai RTA, the MOI e-services and Emirates Vehicle Gate offer vehicle and accident history lookups by chassis number. Abu Dhabi uses its own police and TAMM services. Fees and names change, so check the official sites.
  • For a US or Canada importBuy a history report (such as Carfax or AutoCheck) using the VIN. Look for salvage, flood, rebuilt or total-loss titles, and odometer jumps.
  • Look for flood signsMusty smell, damp or new carpets, rust on seat rails and under the dash, silt in the spare-wheel well, foggy lights, and electrical faults.
  • Get an independent inspectionMany garages and the RTA-approved test centres offer pre-purchase checks. Ask for paint thickness readings, which show repainted panels.
  • Check for loans and finesMake sure any bank mortgage on the car is cleared and fines are paid before transfer.

How the check digit works: each of the 17 characters becomes a number (letters have fixed values, so A=1, B=2 … J=1, S=2), each position has a weight (8, 7, 6 … 10 for position 8), and the weighted sum divided by 11 leaves a remainder that should equal character 9 (10 is written X). One mistyped or altered character almost always breaks it. It is required in North America and China. Many European and Japanese makers don't use it, so a mismatch on those cars is normal.
