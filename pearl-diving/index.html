A pearl diving season.

Before oil, the summer pearl season, الغوص الكبير, was how most coastal families in the Gulf made a living. You are the nakhuda (captain) of a pearling boat in the 1920s. Choose the banks, keep the crew supplied, read the weather, sell your pearls, and share out the season. It was hard, dangerous work, and this game tries to show it honestly.

    About this game

    The season dates, crew roles, share system and the collapse come from the sources below. The pearl prices, catch rates and storm odds are invented to make a playable game. They follow the direction of the history, not real figures. Money is in Indian rupees, which were used across the Gulf at the time.

    The share-out follows a system recorded in Bahrain: after expenses, the nakhuda took a fifth, and the rest was split in shares, three for each diver and two for each puller. Advances taken during the year were then taken off each man's share, so a poor season left debt for the next one. Rules differed from port to port and boat to boat. Here the nahham gets three shares and each boy one, which is a guess.
