What your boarding pass reveals.

The barcode on a boarding pass isn't random. It spells out your name, booking reference, flight, seat and often your frequent flyer and ticket numbers, in plain text that any free scanner app can read. Scan one here to see exactly what a photo of it gives away.

Keep your pass to yourself
  • Don't post photos of boarding passes or bag tags, even with the name covered. The barcode holds the same details.
  • Phone screenshots of mobile passes count too. Crop them out of travel photos.
  • Shred or tear through the barcode on paper passes before throwing them away, including the stubs left in seat pockets and airport bins.
  • Add a PIN or password to your airline account and frequent flyer programme, and turn on sign-in alerts if offered.
  • If a pass was already shared, call the airline. They can add a note to the booking or reissue the reference.

How it works. Airlines follow an industry standard called Bar Coded Boarding Pass (BCBP). It's a fixed layout of letters: positions 3 to 22 are your name, the next 7 are the booking reference, then airports, airline, flight number, the day of the year, cabin and seat. Paper passes use a PDF417 stack barcode; phones use Aztec or QR. The data is not encrypted. Some airlines add a digital signature, which stops forgery but doesn't hide anything.
